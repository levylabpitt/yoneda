Yoneda Store

Take what another yoneda already worked out.

The first time your notebook meets a file format it has never seen, it asks an AI what the format means, and writes the answer down as a permanent part of what it knows. That costs a little money and a few seconds, once. Every file of that kind afterwards is free, because the question has already been answered.

The store is where that answer goes so that nobody else has to buy it again. One group teaches its notebook to read an instrument's files; everyone else takes the result. The more groups do this, the less any one of them pays.

What travels

Only what a format means. That the sample interval of a LabVIEW measurement file lives in a field called wf_increment is a fact about the format, true in every lab, and worth sharing. That your scans sit in a folder named after the student who took them is a fact about your filesystem, useful to you and nobody else, and it stays on your machine.

Nothing about your files is published here. Not their names, not their contents, not where they live, not who made them. The line is drawn at the moment the notebook learns something, and it is drawn by the notebook, not by you remembering to be careful.

    Three commitments

    Nothing here is an authority. What you take from the store arrives as a claim, the same as anything a colleague tells you. Your notebook weighs it against what it already believes. Nothing published here can overwrite what your instance knows.

    Two labs that learn the same thing write the same record. A record is named by a fingerprint of its own content, so agreement needs no coordination. Sharing is not a synchronization problem; identical answers are already the same answer.

    Disagreement is kept. Two groups can publish different readings of one format, because their instruments genuinely differ. Both stay. A store that silently picked a winner would eventually pick the one that was wrong for your rig.

    Why a plasmid

    Every yoneda descends from the same empty starting point and records its own birth; that is inheritance, and it runs in one direction. What the store moves is different. A single self-contained thing, conferring one capability, passing between instances that are not related to each other, which the recipient is free to keep or to drop.

    Bacteria do exactly this, and the package they pass around is called a plasmid. Acquiring one does not change what you descended from. Several versions circulate at once and none of them is the official one. The word is borrowed because it is accurate, not because it is decorative.

    Status

    Nothing is published yet. The store is a real instance with its own beginning, like every other yoneda, and it is empty. The channel that moves plasmids between instances is designed and not yet built. The list above is what is queued, in the order the lab is likely to need it.
