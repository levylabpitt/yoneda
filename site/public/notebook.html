yoneda notebook

A lab notebook for your own computer. It arranges itself around the research you actually do, and it keeps everything you put into it.

installWindows, two clicks

  1. Download the installer, about 11 megabytes.
    YonedaNotebook-Setup.exe · 11 MB
    The notebook is in use inside the lab and is not being handed out more widely yet, so the download lives in the lab's GitHub. Sign in as a lab member and it is the first file under the latest release. It installs under your own Windows account, so a managed university machine will take it without administrator rights or a call to IT.
  2. Run it. Windows may say it does not recognize the program, since it is new and not yet signed. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Your browser opens the notebook, and a small icon appears in the tray next to the clock. Left-click it to bring the notebook back whenever you want it, and right-click for Open and Quit. The notebook runs on your machine and stays there.
  4. Setting up takes about a minute. Point it at a folder of your files and it builds the notebook around them: the forms you fill in, the views you browse, and a short manual written for you. A physicist and a photographer end up with different notebooks.

setupThe AI that designs it

An AI does that design. You can let the notebook call one for you, using a key you supply, or you can copy the prompt it hands you into whatever AI you already use and paste the answer back. Take the second route and nothing leaves your computer at all. Either way, what the AI sees is the names of your files, their types, their folders and their dates. It never sees what is inside them. If you would rather not involve an AI, the notebook can read those same file names itself and set you up without one.

your dataOne file, and nothing thrown away

The whole notebook is a single file on your computer. Your measurements and documents stay where they already sit: logging a four gigabyte data file records where it lives and what it belongs to, and costs you almost nothing. Nothing you enter is ever deleted. When something turns out to be wrong you mark it as withdrawn, and it stays in the record, because a mistake you can still see is worth more than a tidy file you cannot check. Two people who have each kept their own notebook can put them together later, straight from one machine to the other, and the merge takes care of itself.

Mac or Linux?
The Windows installer is the packaged version. Elsewhere you run the program from its source, which needs Python and nothing beyond it.
What happens if I stop using it?
Uninstall from Windows Settings, under Apps. Your notebook file stays where it is. It is an ordinary database file, so what you wrote remains readable without this program, or any program of ours.
Working next to an instrument?
If you want a notebook that watches an instrument and records every file it produces, there is a separate setup for that.
yoneda /start /make
